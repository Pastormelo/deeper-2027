Section 1 | Reference

The vision: DEEPER

Rooted in Christ, so that the gospel shapes more of who we are and how we live.

A tree does not grow taller than its roots can hold. Neither does a church. We have spent years building what people can see: gatherings, teams, a pathway, a building full of ministry. In 2027, we are committing ourselves to what holds all of it up. We want a church of people who are genuinely rooted in Christ, formed by His Word, committed to one another, dependent on the Holy Spirit, and faithful in making disciples.

This is what makes reaching our city sustainable. Shallow roots produce fast growth and quick collapse. Deep roots produce fruit that lasts, in people who keep loving and serving long after the excitement passes.

How we lead this well

Facilitation is not teaching. Opening Scripture, encouraging, praying, and guiding conversation differ from functioning as a theological teacher. Substantial teaching needs its own approval.

We vet the person and the proposal: character, doctrinal alignment, maturity, reliability, teachability, and responsible spiritual influence.

Leaders connect people to the church and its recognized shepherds. They do not cultivate dependency or act as unofficial pastors. "Let me ask a pastor" is a healthy answer.

We communicate simply: what is available, whom it serves, what is expected, how to join. No one memorizes five categories or attends every environment.

Section 5 | How to write your plan

A plan that can be executed

Two good outcomes beat three weak ones.
Honest uncertainty beats fake certainty.

Outcome, not activity

An outcome is a change for people that you could observe by a date. An activity is something you do to help produce it. Write the outcome first; the activities become the first actions.

The outcome sentence

Pattern

By [date], [who] will [observable result], so that [benefit to people].

Then add the baseline today (with its source, or "missing: how we will get it"), the evidence you will look at, and the first action with an owner and due date.

Six questions every draft answers

{{ q.n }} {{ q.t }}

Requests, costs, and dates

Requests of other teams

Name the person, the deliverable and the needed-by date. Ask whether it is realistic. Write Agreed only after they confirm; otherwise Pending. If they are not in the room, it stays pending.

Costs

Every cost is an estimate with a basis (a quote, last year's actual, unit price times count), marked recurring or one-time. Unknown is written as unknown. "None" is an acceptable answer. Shared costs are counted once and named as shared.

Dates

Every date you add is PROPOSED until leadership approves it. Check the 2027 calendar for retained dates and their status before proposing.
